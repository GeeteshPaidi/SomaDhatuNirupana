\section{Forward model}\label{sec:model}

We model the lunar regolith within one footprint as a flat, homogeneous, infinitely thick target. Its composition is parameterised by element-to-silicon weight ratios $r_i = C_i/C_\mathrm{Si}$, and the observed spectrum is the sum of four components: fluorescence lines, scattered solar continuum and lines, particle background, and, where applicable, a solar-driven instrumental component (the primary dayside model fixes it to zero; \cref{sec:background}). All atomic parameters come from xraylib 4.2 \citep{Schoonjans2011}.

\subsection{Composition closure}\label{sec:closure}
The composition is parameterised by Mg/Si, Al/Si, Ca/Si, Ti/Si and Fe/Si. The fit varies Mg/Si, Al/Si and Ca/Si and holds Ti/Si and Fe/Si fixed (\cref{sec:likelihood}). Each element is assumed to be present as its common lunar oxide (SiO$_2$, MgO, Al$_2$O$_3$, CaO, TiO$_2$, FeO). Minor oxides are fixed at typical soil values (Na$_2$O 0.5, K$_2$O 0.1, MnO 0.15, Cr$_2$O$_3$ 0.2 wt\%), close to the average soils of the Apollo and Luna landing sites (Na$_2$O 0.31--0.70, K$_2$O 0.04--0.55, MnO 0.13--0.30, Cr$_2$O$_3$ 0.23--0.36 wt\%; \citealp{Heiken1991}, Table 7.15). Varying them over these ranges changes $C_\mathrm{Si}$ in \cref{eq:closure} by less than 1\%. Requiring the oxides to sum to unity fixes the absolute Si weight fraction:
\begin{equation}
C_\mathrm{Si} = \frac{1 - \sum_m w_m^\mathrm{ox}}{f_\mathrm{Si} + \sum_i r_i f_i},
\label{eq:closure}
\end{equation}
where $f_i$ is the element-to-oxide mass factor (oxide mass per unit mass of the element, e.g.\ 2.14 for Si) and $w_m^\mathrm{ox}$ are the fixed minor-oxide fractions. Oxygen follows stoichiometrically, so it is not a free parameter. The matrix mass attenuation coefficient is $\mu(E)=\sum_j C_j\,\mu_j(E)$, with $\mu_j$ the total mass attenuation coefficient of element $j$.

\subsection{Incident solar spectrum}\label{sec:solar}
The incident photon spectrum $S(E;T)$ is the isothermal CHIANTI spectrum, lines plus continuum with coronal abundances \citep{Dere1997}, evaluated with \texttt{sunkit-spex} at the GOES temperature of each exposure (\cref{sec:goes}). It is tabulated on a \SI{5}{eV} grid from \SIrange{1.01}{20}{keV} at 61 logarithmically spaced temperatures between 2 and \SI{40}{MK}, and interpolated in $\log T$. Its absolute normalisation is absorbed by a free amplitude.

Line emission dominates the excitation of the light elements. Between 3 and \SI{15}{MK}, \LineFracMgLo--\LineFracMgHi\% of the photon flux above the Mg K edge, and \LineFracSiLo--\LineFracSiHi\% above the Si K edge, is in lines, mainly the He-like Mg\,\textsc{xi}, Si\,\textsc{xiii} and S\,\textsc{xv} complexes. A bremsstrahlung-only spectrum would therefore misrepresent the relative excitation of Mg, Al and Si (\cref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Primary fluorescence}\label{sec:primary}
For line $\ell$ (K$\alpha$ or K$\beta$) of element $i$, the primary fluorescence intensity follows \citet{Sherman1955}:
\begin{equation}
P_{i\ell} = C_i\,\omega_{i,K}\,p_{i\ell}\int_{E_{K,i}}^{E_\mathrm{max}}
\frac{S(E;T)\,\tau_{i,K}(E)}{\mu(E)/\sin\psi_1 + \mu(E_{i\ell})/\sin\psi_2}\,\mathrm{d}E ,
\label{eq:primary}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{i,K}$ is the K-shell photoionisation cross-section, $\omega_{i,K}$ is the K-shell fluorescence yield, $p_{i\ell}$ is the radiative rate of the line, and $E_{K,i}$ is the K-edge energy. The incidence elevation is $\psi_1 = 90^\circ - \texttt{SOLARANG}$, and the emergence elevation is $\psi_2=90^\circ$ (nadir). The product $\omega_{i,K}p_{i\ell}\tau_{i,K}$ reproduces the xraylib fluorescence-line cross-section to within 5\%. The integral is evaluated on the \SI{5}{eV} grid up to $E_\mathrm{max}=\SI{15}{keV}$; a unit test shows this agrees with adaptive quadrature to within 1\% and with a \SI{1}{eV} grid to within 0.3\%. Because the integrand depends on the composition through $\mu$, \cref{eq:primary} couples all elements. This coupling is the matrix effect; ratios of line intensities are therefore not equal to ratios of concentrations.

\subsection{Secondary fluorescence}\label{sec:secondary}
Fluorescence of element $i$ excited by line $m$ of element $j$ ($E_{jm}>E_{K,i}$) is computed with the thick-target expression of \citet{deBoer1990}, which is linear in both $C_i$ and $C_j$. Over \SIrange{3}{15}{MK} and both mare and highland compositions, the secondary contribution to every K$\alpha$ line is at most \SecMaxPct\% of the primary. We therefore omit it from the fitted model. We do, however, include it when generating the synthetic spectra of \cref{sec:synthetic}, so the bias from omitting it is part of the measured recovery bias.

\subsection{Scattered solar spectrum}\label{sec:scatter}
Coherent and incoherent scattering of the solar spectrum by the regolith contributes a continuum, and at the GOES temperatures encountered, scattered solar lines as well:
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(E) = S(E;T)\,\frac{\sigma_s(E)}{\mu(E)/\sin\psi_1 + \mu(E)/\sin\psi_2},
\label{eq:scatter}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_s=\sum_j C_j(\sigma_{\mathrm{coh},j}+\sigma_{\mathrm{inc},j})$. The angular dependence of the differential scattering cross-sections is not modelled. Instead, the scattered component carries its own free amplitude $A_\mathrm{sc}$ (\cref{eq:model}).

\subsection{Detector response and energy scale}\label{sec:detector}\label{sec:calib}
The centre energy of channel $k$ is $E_k=g\,k+o$. Each line is distributed over channels by a Gaussian integrated over the channel boundaries $E_k^\pm=E_k\pm g/2$:
\begin{equation}
R_{\ell k} = \frac{\Phi\!\left(\frac{E_k^+-E_\ell}{\sigma_\ell}\right)-\Phi\!\left(\frac{E_k^--E_\ell}{\sigma_\ell}\right)}{\mathrm{erf}(5/\sqrt{2})},
\qquad \sigma_\ell^2=\sigma_0^2+F\,w\,E_\ell ,
\label{eq:response}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi$ is the standard normal cumulative distribution, truncated at $\pm5\sigma$, $F=0.115$ is the Fano factor of silicon and $w=\SI{3.68}{eV}$. The normalisation conserves line area to $10^{-9}$ (unit test). The scattered spectrum, \cref{eq:scatter}, is folded through the same response on the \SI{5}{eV} grid. The fit therefore uses this Gaussian redistribution, with a width that depends on detector temperature, together with the distributed ancillary response (below). A response matrix is also distributed in the PRADAN calibration bundle (\cref{sec:class}); we did not use it and have not compared fits made with it against this calibrated Gaussian.

The gain $g$, offset $o$ and noise term $\sigma_0$ are determined from the data. We stack all 16-SCD dayside exposures, subtract the particle background, and fit the Mg, Al, Si and Ca K lines jointly. For December 2024 as a whole (\NStackDec{} exposures) we find $g=\SI{\GainDec}{eV/channel}$, $o=\SI{\OffsetDec}{eV}$ and $\sigma_0=\SI{\SigmaZeroDec}{eV}$, i.e.\ \SI{\FwhmAlDec}{eV} FWHM at Al K$\alpha$. The fitted gain is \GainPctDec\% larger than the header value of \SI{13.5}{eV/channel}; using the header gain shifts the Si K$\alpha$ centroid by about two channels.

The line width depends strongly on the detector temperature (\texttt{TEMP}). Within December 2024, \texttt{TEMP} spans $-49$ to $-22$\,\textdegree C, and the Al K$\alpha$ FWHM rises from \SI{136}{eV} at $-46$\,\textdegree C to \SI{304}{eV} at $-25$\,\textdegree C, while the gain is stable to 0.7\% and the offset to \SI{\pm10}{eV}. Because \texttt{TEMP} follows the orbit (correlation 0.89 with latitude in that month), a single monthly $\sigma_0$ would turn this into a latitude-dependent bias. We therefore split each month's stack into up to eight \texttt{TEMP} quantile bins (at least 8000 spectra per bin), fit $g$, $o$ and $\sigma_0$ in each, and interpolate the three parameters linearly to the \texttt{TEMP} of each spectrum (clamped at the ends). Months with fewer spectra use a single calibration. \Cref{tab:months} lists the temperature and FWHM ranges per month, and \cref{fig:fwhmtemp} shows the line width against temperature for all months: at a fixed temperature the FWHM increases by about \SI{7}{eV} per year, as expected from radiation damage.

The energy dependence of the efficiency is taken from the ancillary response distributed in the PRADAN calibration bundle (\texttt{class\_arf\_v1.arf}), applied at the photon energy before folding. It includes the two optical-blocking filters (each \SI{0.2}{\micro m} polyimide coated with \SI{0.2}{\micro m} aluminium; \citealp{Radhakrishna2020,Pillai2021}), collimator blocking and absorption in the devices. Only its shape enters the fit: the absolute area, and its scaling with the number of SCDs summed, is absorbed by the free amplitude. Relative to Si K$\alpha$, the ARF efficiency is 1.02 at Mg K$\alpha$ and 1.25 at Al K$\alpha$, because the Al K edge (\SI{1.56}{keV}) of the filter coating lies between the Al and Si lines. Compared with the transmission of the filters alone, the ARF lowers the relative Mg:Si efficiency by a factor 0.82 and the Al:Si efficiency by 0.95. To first order, inferred from these efficiency ratios rather than from refits, this raises the retrieved Mg/Si by about 22\% and Al/Si by about 5\%.

\subsection{Background: particles and a solar-driven instrumental component}\label{sec:background}
Nightside CLASS spectra (\texttt{SOLARANG} $\geq 90^\circ$) contain two distinguishable components (\cref{fig:background}).

The first is the particle background, a smooth continuum with weak features. It dominates exposures taken while the spacecraft is in the lunar umbra, i.e.\ when \texttt{SOLARANG} exceeds $90^\circ+\arccos[R_\mathrm{M}/(R_\mathrm{M}+h)]\approx\SI{109}{\degree}$ at $h=\SI{100}{km}$. The second is a strong Al K$\alpha$ line, together with a soft continuum and a feature near \SI{6.7}{keV}. This second component appears only while the spacecraft itself is sunlit. In December 2024 the median background-corrected Al K$\alpha$ rate of nightside exposures is \SI{\AlSunlit}{counts\,s^{-1}} when the spacecraft is sunlit ($n=\NSunlitDec$), against \SI{\AlUmbra}{counts\,s^{-1}} in the umbra ($n=\NUmbraDec$). Its rate correlates with solar proxies (Spearman $\rho=\RhoAlSoft$ with the \SIrange{0.6}{0.9}{keV} counts) but not with the \SIrange{8}{20}{keV} particle rate ($\rho=\RhoAlHard$; this diagnostic, and \cref{fig:background}, use the \SIrange{8}{20}{keV} band of an earlier stage of the analysis, not the \SIrange{8}{17}{keV} band adopted for the fits, \cref{sec:likelihood}), and it varies on time scales shorter than \SI{30}{min}. We interpret it as solar X-ray fluorescence of aluminium in the instrument (filters and structure). In sunlit nightside spectra it is therefore significant; were it present on the dayside at the same level, it would be comparable in magnitude to the lunar Al line.

We therefore build two templates per month, in units of counts\,s$^{-1}$ per 16 SCDs. The first is $b^{\mathrm{p}}_k$, the mean umbra spectrum. The second is $b^{\mathrm{s}}_k$, the mean sunlit-nightside spectrum minus $b^{\mathrm{p}}_k$ scaled to the same \SIrange{8}{17}{keV} rate. Months with fewer than 100 umbra (or sunlit) nightside exposures use the December 2024 template shape. We could not predict the instrumental amplitude from GOES with useful accuracy: the GOES-predicted Al-exciting flux explains it only to a factor of $\approx$2 (Spearman $\rho=\RhoAlGoes$), with an angle-dependent scale.

The geometry of \cref{fig:background} indicates when the component arises. For dayside nadir-pointing observations (\texttt{SOLARANG} $<90^\circ$), the Sun is above the plane of the instrument's Moon-facing (aperture) side, i.e.\ behind the instrument relative to its viewing direction, and that side, including the outer filter, is in the shadow of the spacecraft. Only when the Sun is below that plane (\texttt{SOLARANG} $>90^\circ$) and the spacecraft is still sunlit ($\lesssim\SI{109}{\degree}$) can sunlight reach the aperture side directly. The observed onset near 93\textdegree{} and the disappearance at the shadow boundary agree with this geometry. We therefore expect the component to be negligible on the dayside, although it is significant in sunlit nightside spectra. Our primary model therefore fixes its amplitude to $\gamma=0$ for all dayside spectra, so the background is $\beta\,b^{\mathrm{p}}_k$. We also ran an alternative model in which $\gamma$ is a free, non-negative parameter. It is reported in \cref{sec:alt} as a sensitivity test.
