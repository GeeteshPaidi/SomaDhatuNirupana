\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The major-element chemistry of the lunar surface records how the crust formed and evolved. Highland plagioclase is rich in Al and Ca; mare basalts are rich in Fe, Mg and Ti. Global maps of these elements constrain magma-ocean crystallisation, mare volcanism and impact mixing \citep{Heiken1991}. Orbital gamma-ray spectroscopy has provided global maps of Fe, Ti, Th and K, and model-dependent estimates of Mg, Al, Si and Ca, at a resolution of hundreds of kilometres \citep{Lawrence1998,Prettyman2006}.

X-ray fluorescence (XRF) excited by solar X-rays offers better spatial resolution and direct sensitivity to Mg, Al and Si \citep{Jenkins1999,Beckhoff2006}. The Chandrayaan-1 X-ray Spectrometer (C1XS) demonstrated the technique during a period of very low solar activity, when only a few flares gave usable spectra \citep{Grande2009,Narendranath2011}. Its successor on Chandrayaan-2, the Large Area Soft X-ray Spectrometer (CLASS), has a geometric area of \SI{64}{cm^2} and a footprint of a few tens of kilometres \citep{Narendranath2014,Radhakrishna2020,Pillai2021}. It has operated through the rise of solar cycle 25, and its Level-1 spectra are public.

Turning XRF line intensities into composition requires the spectrum of the incident solar X-rays. The fluorescence line flux of each element depends on the solar flux above its K absorption edge, so a change in the solar spectrum can change line-flux ratios such as Mg/Si or Ca/Si even when the surface composition is unchanged. The Chandrayaan-2 Solar X-ray Monitor (XSM) measures this spectrum \citep{Mithun2020}, and the instrument team's analyses combine CLASS spectra with simultaneous XSM data, building on the fundamental-parameter abundance algorithm developed for C1XS \citep{Athiray2013a,Athiray2013b}. With this approach, \citet{Narendranath2024} derived global maps of Mg, Al, Si, Ca and Fe from three years of CLASS data, and \citet{Narendranath2025} summarise five years of CLASS results; maps of X-ray line-flux ratios have also been presented \citep{Kumar2026}. Global Mg/Si and Al/Si maps were first derived from the Chang'E-2 X-ray spectrometer \citep{Dong2016}, from observations selected at a GOES-derived solar temperature of \SI{4}{MK}, as molar ratios on a 20\textdegree{}$\times$20\textdegree{} equal-area grid normalised to Lunar Prospector GRS; and oxide maps calibrated against returned samples, including those of Chang'E-5, have been derived from Kaguya Multiband Imager reflectance \citep{Wang2021,Zhang2023}. For users who work only with the CLASS archive, a common simplification is to assume a reference solar spectrum or to rely on line ratios, although ratios still depend on the solar spectrum because each element samples a different part of it.

In this paper we develop and test a CLASS processing chain that can be reproduced from public data. Each spectrum is fitted on raw counts with a simplified fundamental-parameter model, in which the solar excitation of each exposure is an isothermal CHIANTI spectrum at the temperature derived from the GOES X-Ray Sensor. Synthetic recovery tests establish which ratios this approach can constrain, and a solar-driven instrumental Al K$\alpha$ component seen in sunlit nightside spectra is taken into account in the background model.
Building on earlier global Mg/Si and Al/Si mapping with Chang'E-2 and on CLASS studies of elemental abundances and line-flux ratios, we use GOES to estimate the incident spectrum separately for each qualifying CLASS exposure across a range of solar temperatures, and test what can be recovered in this way. We assess the repeatability of the resulting elemental weight-ratio maps across two observing periods and refit overlapping CLASS exposures using XSM-derived solar spectra. The maps reproduce the major spatial patterns across periods, while the XSM comparison shows that Mg/Si is more sensitive than Al/Si to the assumed solar spectrum. We report element ratios relative to Si, not absolute abundances, and we state which quantities the data do not constrain with this approach. The maps are intended to complement, not replace, the XSM-based analyses of the instrument team.
