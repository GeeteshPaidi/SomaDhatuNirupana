\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

We have described a CLASS processing chain that uses only public data. It combines a simplified fundamental-parameter model, a CHIANTI solar spectrum at the GOES temperature of each exposure, the energy dependence of the distributed ancillary response, a detector calibration that follows the detector temperature, a particle-background template and a Poisson likelihood fit with per-spectrum uncertainties and quality flags. Applied to \NRawFiles{} Level-1 exposures from \NMonths{} monthly archives, it gives \NMapped{} quality-controlled spectra and global 2\textdegree{} maps of Mg/Si and Al/Si with complete observed coverage. Our main findings are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Both maps are repeatable. Maps built separately from February 2022 -- December 2023 (\EpBNFp{} spectra) and November 2024 -- May 2026 correlate at $r=\EpMgTwoR$ (Mg/Si) and \EpAlTwoR{} (Al/Si) on common 2\textdegree{} cells, with median levels within 1\%, under different solar conditions and detector line widths.
\item Al/Si correlates with Lunar Prospector GRS ($r=\GrsAlR$) and with nine landing-site soils ($r=\SiteAlTwoR$), and several maria appear as distinct units. Mg/Si correlates with GRS ($r=\GrsMgR$) and with the soils ($r=\SiteMgTwoR$), with a wider confidence interval and a stronger dependence on the Apollo 16 site. Both ratios are systematically higher than the soil values, for reasons not yet resolved, so the maps are relative.
\item The retrieved ratios depend on the incident solar spectrum, and Mg/Si much more than Al/Si. Solar line emission carries most of the flux that excites Mg, Al and Si. With a GOES-based spectrum the spatial patterns of Mg/Si and Al/Si are recovered, whereas Ca/Si, Ti/Si and Fe/Si are not constrained. For three months, replacing the GOES-based spectrum by XSM-derived spectra shifts the median Al/Si by a few per cent and Mg/Si by \XsmTwoMg{} to \XsmOneMg\%, depending on the assumed thermal structure; the solar excitation is a major source of Mg/Si uncertainty.
\item CLASS nightside spectra contain a solar-driven instrumental Al K$\alpha$ component while the spacecraft is sunlit and the Sun can illuminate the instrument's Moon-facing side. It is expected to be negligible on the dayside, but its treatment in the background model affects Al/Si.
\end{enumerate}
The code, mapping-set results and maps are public. Extending the XSM-based treatment to all months, with free coronal abundances, is the most direct way to reduce the remaining systematic uncertainty.
