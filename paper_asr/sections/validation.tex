\section{Validation}\label{sec:validation}

The following choices were fixed and logged before the validation of the primary model was run:
\begin{itemize}
\item the matching radii (1, 2 and 5\textdegree, with 2\textdegree{} primary);
\item the statistics: Pearson and Spearman coefficients with bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals, leave-one-site-out ranges, and a 20\textdegree{} spatial block bootstrap for GRS;
\item the criterion for regarding an element as supported by both comparisons: Pearson $r>0$ with a 95\% confidence interval excluding zero in both the landing-site and GRS tests.
\end{itemize}
The history of the primary model, including an earlier model whose validation motivated it, is given in \cref{sec:alt}.

\subsection{Landing sites}\label{sec:sites}
\Cref{tab:sites} lists the returned-soil ratios (average soil compositions; \citealp{Heiken1991}, Table 7.15; \citealp{Basu1998}) and the CLASS weighted means within 2\textdegree{} of each site.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Al/Si, 2\textdegree:} $r=\SiteAlTwoR$ (95\% CI \SiteAlTwoCI), Spearman $\rho=\SiteAlTwoRho$ ($p=\SiteAlTwoRhoP$), leave-one-out range \SiteAlTwoLooLo--\SiteAlTwoLooHi.
\item \emph{Al/Si, other radii:} $r=\SiteAlOneR$ (CI \SiteAlOneCI) at 1\textdegree{} and $r=\SiteAlFiveR$ at 5\textdegree.
\item \emph{Mg/Si, 2\textdegree:} $r=\SiteMgTwoR$ (\SiteMgTwoCI); $r=\SiteMgOneR$ at 1\textdegree{} and $\SiteMgFiveR$ at 5\textdegree.
\end{itemize}
The CLASS site means have small statistical errors (\cref{tab:sites}). Their scatter about the soil values is therefore mostly systematic. Footprints of $25\times37$\,km average over terrain much larger than the sampled landing sites, and several sites lie on mare--highland contacts (Apollo 15, 16, 17; Luna 20). Al/Si meets the site criterion; Mg/Si does not.

\subsection{Lunar Prospector GRS}\label{sec:grs}
All \GrsMgN{} 5\textdegree{} equal-area GRS pixels contain at least 20 CLASS footprints. On these pixels:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Al/Si:} $r=\GrsAlR$ (block-bootstrap 95\% CI \GrsAlCI; Spearman $\rho=\GrsAlRho$);
\item \emph{Mg/Si:} $r=\GrsMgR$ (\GrsMgCI; $\rho=\GrsMgRho$).
\end{itemize}
Both meet the GRS criterion (\cref{fig:validation}). The correlations are stable under the count-threshold test: Al/Si $r=\GrsAlR$, \GrsAlRMinK{} and \GrsAlRMinKKK, and Mg/Si $r=\GrsMgR$, \GrsMgRMinK{} and \GrsMgRMinKKK, for thresholds of 300, 1000 and 3000 counts (\NMapped, \NMappedMinK{} and \NMappedMinKKK{} spectra). Restricting to GOES $\geq$ B3 leaves them unchanged.

\subsection{Interpretation of the correlation values}\label{sec:limits_scores}
\emph{Agreement with GRS is bounded by noise in both data sets.} We split the mapping set into two halves by alternate months, map each half separately, and correlate the two maps. After Spearman--Brown correction, the CLASS pixel means have reliabilities of \RelAl{} (Al/Si) and \RelMg{} (Mg/Si). The GRS error matrices imply reliabilities of \RelGrsAl{} and \RelGrsMg. The maximum attainable correlations are therefore $\sqrt{\rho_\mathrm{CLASS}\rho_\mathrm{GRS}}=\CeilAl$ for Al/Si and \CeilMg{} for Mg/Si. The observed values are about 80\% of these ceilings. The GRS Mg and Al abundances are themselves model-dependent \citep{Prettyman2006}.

\emph{The landing-site test has little discriminating power at this scale.} Applied to the same nine soil averages, using the 5\textdegree{} pixel containing each site, GRS itself gives $r=\GrsSitesAl$ for Al/Si and \GrsSitesMg{} for Mg/Si. Soil averages sample metres to kilometres around each landing point, whereas orbital pixels average tens to hundreds of kilometres \citep{Basu1998}. With nine sites and this mismatch in scale, the site test has limited power, and its outcome for Mg/Si should be interpreted with caution.

\emph{Comparison with the earlier processing.} The earlier processing of the same data (centre-point binning of denoised least-squares fits without a solar model) gives $r=\OldGrsAl$ (Al/Si) and \OldGrsMg{} (Mg/Si) on the same GRS pixels.

\emph{Weighted median.} Replacing the weighted mean by a weighted median changes little: GRS $r=\MedGrsAl$ (Al/Si) and \MedGrsMg{} (Mg/Si). \Cref{fig:posthoc} shows the weighted-median maps.

\subsection{Alternative model and the history of the primary model}\label{sec:alt}
The analysis was first run with the instrumental amplitude $\gamma$ free (alternative model, \NMappedvTwo{} mapped spectra). Under the same pre-declared rules, that model gave the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{GRS:} Al/Si $r=\GrsAlRvTwo$ (\GrsAlCIvTwo) and Mg/Si $r=\GrsMgRvTwo$ (\GrsMgCIvTwo).
\item \emph{Landing sites (2\textdegree):} Al/Si $r=\SiteAlTwoRvTwo$ and Mg/Si \SiteMgTwoRvTwo. Neither element was claimed under the criterion.
\item \emph{Diagnosis:} the Al/Si reliability was only \RelAlvTwo{} (ceiling \CeilAlvTwo). In a fixed farside region, the monthly median Al/Si anticorrelated with the fraction of spectra having a non-zero fitted $\gamma$ (Spearman $-0.88$): in some months the free $\gamma$ absorbed part of the lunar Al line.
\item \emph{Post-hoc check:} restricting that model to spectra with $\gamma<0.05$ (\GzPct\%) gave GRS Al/Si $r=\GzAlR$.
\end{itemize}

Because the nightside geometry (\cref{fig:background}) suggests, independently of the reference data, that the component is negligible on the dayside, we then specified the primary model ($\gamma\equiv0$ on the dayside). We logged it, together with the commitment to report its outcome whatever it was, \emph{before} running it on the full data set. All other choices are identical to the alternative model.

During development we also corrected one defect after a first validation. The boundary threshold used to flag pinned ratios was raised from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-3}$, because 254 fits pinned near zero had collapsed Fisher uncertainties and dominated the inverse-variance weights. Before that fix, the alternative model gave GRS Al/Si $r=\GrsAlRvOne$.

The primary model was thus motivated by the diagnosis of the alternative model's validation, and we note this so that readers can weigh it accordingly. Its specification was fixed before it was run, and it rests on a physical argument that does not involve the reference data.

\subsection{Outcome}
Under the pre-declared rule, Al/Si meets both criteria: GRS ($r=\GrsAlR$) and the landing sites ($r=\SiteAlTwoR$). Mg/Si meets the GRS criterion ($r=\GrsMgR$) but not the nine-site criterion, which GRS itself also does not meet. We therefore present Al/Si as a relative map that is supported by both comparisons, and Mg/Si as a relative map whose large-scale pattern agrees with GRS, without a claim of site-level accuracy.
