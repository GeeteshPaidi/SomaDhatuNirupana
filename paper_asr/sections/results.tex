\subsection{Pipeline statistics}\label{sec:selection}
\Cref{tab:stages} traces the exposures through the pipeline. All \NDayside{} dayside exposures with valid geometry were fitted; no flux-percentile pre-selection was applied. \NMapped{} spectra (\PctMapped\% of the dayside set) pass every quality criterion and form the mapping set. The fraction passing varies strongly with solar activity. It ranges from \PassMinPct\% in \PassMinMonth{}, shortly after solar minimum, where most spectra fall below the count threshold or below GOES B1, to \PassMaxPct\% in \PassMaxMonth{}, with a median of \PassMedPct\% over the \NMonths{} months (\cref{tab:months}). The GOES temperatures of the mapped exposures have a median of \MapTmed{}\,MK (5th--95th percentile \MapTlo--\MapThi{}\,MK).

\subsection{Fit examples}\label{sec:examples}
\Cref{fig:fits} shows fits to a bright, a median and a faint dayside spectrum. The model describes the Mg, Al and Si K lines, the Ca K line and the scattered solar continuum and lines. In the faint spectrum the particle background dominates above \SI{2}{keV}, and the uncertainties on Mg/Si and Al/Si become correspondingly large.  Bright flare spectra (e.g.\ panel (a), $T=16.8$\,MK) often fail the goodness-of-fit criterion, whereas faint spectra fail mainly the count threshold or the GOES B1 cut. Residual structure below \SI{2}{keV} points to limitations of the isothermal solar spectrum and of the line-shape model, so the mapping set is weighted towards non-flaring and moderate-flare conditions.

\Cref{fig:spectrasites} shows the two brightest qualifying (quality-0) spectra within 0.5\textdegree{} of each of two locations analysed by \citet{Narendranath2024}, (57.51\textdegree N, 78.14\textdegree E) and (30.52\textdegree S, 132.46\textdegree W). \subsection{Global maps}\label{sec:maps}
\Cref{fig:maps} shows the Mg/Si and Al/Si maps with their observation-count and standard-error layers. Over the mapping set, the median footprint ratios are Mg/Si $=\MedMg$ (5th--95th percentile \PfiveMg--\PninetyfiveMg) and Al/Si $=\MedAl$ (\PfiveAl--\PninetyfiveAl). The median Mg/Si lies within the range of returned lunar soils, while the upper tail extends above it. The Al/Si median is higher than most returned soils, which may indicate a residual efficiency or calibration offset between the Al and Si lines (\cref{sec:limitations}). An offset of this kind would scale all values without changing the spatial pattern or the correlations, so we regard the maps as relative.

Quality-0 footprints reach every 2\textdegree{} cell (\CovUsedCells\% of cells, \CovUsedArea\% of the area), and \CovFiveArea\% of the surface is covered by cells with at least five footprints. The coverage comes from the polar orbits of the 24 archives at all local times, not from interpolation. The polar cells have the fewest footprints and the largest standard errors.

The Mg/Si map shows the expected first-order dichotomy: higher values over the nearside maria and the South Pole--Aitken basin, lower values over the farside feldspathic highlands. The Al/Si map shows the complementary pattern at higher contrast. Several maria (Imbrium, Serenitatis, Crisium, Tranquillitatis, Fecunditatis, Nubium, Humorum) and the South Pole--Aitken basin appear as low-Al/Si units against the feldspathic highlands. Residual north--south striping follows individual orbits. It is consistent with orbit-to-orbit errors in the solar spectrum that the GOES isothermal temperature does not capture (\cref{sec:discussion}), although we have not isolated its cause.

The broad contrast between the Mg-rich maria and the Al-rich highlands is consistent with the Chang'E-2 maps of \citet{Dong2016}. Because those maps are normalised to Lunar Prospector GRS and given on a 20\textdegree{} grid, we do not use them as an independent numerical validation. The Kaguya reflectance products of \citet{Wang2021} and \citet{Zhang2023} provide complementary oxide maps, including MgO and Al$_2$O$_3$, whereas our products are elemental weight ratios relative to Si.
