\section{Spectral fitting}\label{sec:fitting}

\subsection{Likelihood}\label{sec:likelihood}
Each exposure is fitted on its raw counts $d_k$ without smoothing, denoising or background subtraction. The fitted channels are those with $E_k\in[1.15,7.5]$\,keV. The lower limit avoids the Na K$\alpha$ line at \SI{1.04}{keV} and a steep excess below \SI{1.1}{keV} that is present in every stacked spectrum. One additional bin holds the summed counts in \SIrange{8}{17}{keV}, where no lunar fluorescence is expected. That bin helps to constrain the particle-background level without depending on the detailed background shape; the model also predicts the scattered solar counts in it. In the summed dayside spectra of December 2024 and June 2025, the saturation (last-channel) events of the individual SCDs appear after gain alignment as peaks at 17.4--\SI{22.5}{keV}; the \SI{17}{keV} upper limit excludes the saturation peaks observed in these two stacks. The \SIrange{8}{20}{keV} band of the background diagnostics in \cref{sec:background} and \cref{fig:background} belongs to an earlier stage of the analysis and is not the band used in the fits. The expected counts in this bin are \cref{eq:model} summed over its channels: the particle template summed over the band, and the scattered solar spectrum integrated over \SIrange{8}{17}{keV} on the \SI{5}{eV} grid with the efficiency applied; no fluorescence line of the model lies in the band. The expected counts are
\begin{equation}
m_k = A\Big[\sum_\ell \frac{\varepsilon(E_\ell)\,P_\ell}{P_\mathrm{ref}} R_{\ell k} + A_\mathrm{sc}\,\frac{\big(\mathcal{F}[\varepsilon\Sigma]\big)_k}{\Sigma_\mathrm{ref}}\Big]
+ \beta\,b^{\mathrm{p}}_k\,t\,\frac{n_\mathrm{SCD}}{16},
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
where $t$ is the exposure, $n_\mathrm{SCD}$ is the number of SCDs summed, $\varepsilon(E)$ is the relative efficiency from the ancillary response (\cref{sec:calib}), applied at the photon energy, i.e.\ at each line energy and to the scattered spectrum on its \SI{5}{eV} grid before folding, $\mathcal{F}$ is the detector folding of \cref{eq:response}, and $P_\mathrm{ref}$, $\Sigma_\mathrm{ref}$ are fixed reference normalisations. Here $A$ is the overall amplitude, $A_\mathrm{sc}$ the relative amplitude of the scattered solar spectrum (\cref{eq:scatter}) and $\beta$ the particle-background scale; the GOES flux classes (B1, B3) quoted elsewhere are unrelated. The parameter vector is $\theta=(A,r_\mathrm{Mg},r_\mathrm{Al},r_\mathrm{Ca},A_\mathrm{sc},\beta)$. In the alternative model of \cref{sec:alt}, the term $\gamma\,b^{\mathrm{s}}_k\,t\,n_\mathrm{SCD}/16$ is added with $\gamma\ge0$ free. All components are non-negative, the ratios are bounded to $[0,5]$, and $\beta\le10$.

Si is the reference element, so the composition enters only through ratios to Si, with a single overall amplitude. This avoids the scale degeneracy of fitting absolute concentrations together with a free global scale. Ti/Si and Fe/Si are held at global lunar-soil means (0.04 and 0.37) and enter only through the matrix. At GOES temperatures of 3--\SI{8}{MK} the solar flux above the Ti and Fe K edges is too small for an \SI{8}{s} spectrum to constrain them; leaving them free made 92\% of test fits ill-conditioned. We minimise the Cash statistic \citep{Cash1979}
\begin{equation}
C = 2\sum_k \big[m_k - d_k + d_k\ln(d_k/m_k)\big]
\label{eq:cash}
\end{equation}
with a trust-region reflective least-squares solver applied to the signed deviance residuals
\begin{equation}
\delta_k = \mathrm{sign}(d_k-m_k)\,\sqrt{2\big[m_k-d_k+d_k\ln(d_k/m_k)\big]},
\label{eq:devres}
\end{equation}
whose sum of squares equals $C$ (the logarithmic term is zero for $d_k=0$). The residuals plotted in \cref{fig:fits} are these $\delta_k$.

\subsection{Uncertainties, goodness of fit and quality flags}\label{sec:uncert}
Parameter covariances are taken from the inverse Poisson Fisher matrix $\mathcal{I}=J^\top\mathrm{diag}(1/m)J$, where $J=\partial m/\partial\theta$ is computed by finite differences at the optimum. We also record the correlation matrix and the condition number of its core block $(A,r_\mathrm{Mg},r_\mathrm{Al},A_\mathrm{sc},\beta)$. For goodness of fit we compare $C$ over the main window with its expectation and variance for the fitted model, computed exactly for Poisson counts \citep{Kaastra2017}: $z=(C-\langle C\rangle)/\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}\,C}$.

A spectrum enters the maps (quality $=0$) only if all of the following hold:
\begin{itemize}
\item the fit converged;
\item there are at least 300 counts in the \SIrange{1.15}{7.5}{keV} window;
\item $|z|\le3$;
\item neither Mg/Si nor Al/Si lies within $10^{-3}$ of a bound (0 or 5);
\item the core condition number is below $10^4$;
\item the geometry is valid ($0.5^\circ<\psi_1\le 90^\circ$, altitude $>0$);
\item a reliable GOES temperature exists (flux $\ge$ B1).
\end{itemize}
The count threshold, the $|z|$ and condition-number limits, the GOES B1 cut, the 2\textdegree{} mapping grid and the validation radii and statistics were fixed before any comparison with reference data. Two choices were changed during development, after a first comparison with the alternative model of \cref{sec:alt}: the bound tolerance was raised from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-3}$, because fits pinned near zero had collapsed Fisher uncertainties and dominated the inverse-variance weights, and the instrumental amplitude was fixed to zero on the dayside (\cref{sec:background}). All choices were fixed before the primary model was evaluated against the reference data. The count threshold is varied (300, 1000, 3000) as a sensitivity test. No flux-percentile selection is applied.

\subsection{Products}\label{sec:products}
Ca/Si is fitted because it shapes the model between the Si and Ca lines, but it is not reported. The synthetic tests of \cref{sec:synthetic} show it is biased even at the correct temperature and changes by an order of magnitude under realistic temperature errors. The products are Mg/Si and Al/Si elemental weight ratios, $r_i=C_i/C_\mathrm{Si}$, with per-footprint uncertainties. They are not line-flux ratios: the fit converts the observed line fluxes into concentrations through the forward model of \cref{sec:model}, which includes the solar spectrum of each exposure, the incidence geometry, matrix effects and the instrument response. Line-flux ratios are not used at any stage of the mapping or the validation.
